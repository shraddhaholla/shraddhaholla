Research Project

Pecking Order Theory in Corporate Financing Decisions

Amazon vs Coca-Cola

A comparative analysis of financing choices across FY2021–FY2025.

Project Overview

The study examines operating cash flow, capital expenditure, borrowings, dividends and share-related activity to assess how closely the two companies’ financing choices align with the Pecking Order Theory.

Financial measures are presented in US$ billion. The analysis distinguishes outstanding debt from new borrowing and employee share activity from public equity issues.

Understanding the Theory

The financing hierarchy proposed by the theory.

01Internal funds
02Debt financing
03Equity financing

The Pecking Order Theory, introduced by Myers and Majluf (1984), suggests that companies generally prefer internally generated funds first, followed by debt, and then equity. This hierarchy is influenced by information asymmetry between management and outside investors.

The theory is a useful framework, not a strict rule. Financing choices also depend on cash availability, investment needs, profitability and business conditions.
